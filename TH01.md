# TH01 temperature and humidity sensor

A battery Wi-Fi temperature/humidity sensor, board silkscreen `TH01Y-CB3S V2.1`.
Sold under enough names (UanTii, TH01, "T&H Sensor") that the silkscreen is the
only identifier worth trusting.

**Status: solved, both routes.** Wire flash and cloudcutter both work. Three
units run OpenBeken and report to Home Assistant over MQTT.

This is a different class of device from the PC191HA plugs. There is no mains
hazard, no metering, and no LAN OTA path. What makes it interesting is a second
MCU that owns the Wi-Fi module's serial line and its power.

## What is on the board

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| CB3S module (BK7231N) | Wi-Fi and nothing else. Talks to the MCU on UART1 at 9600. |
| U3, an unmarked SOIC-16 MCU | Reads the sensor, owns the power, decides when the Wi-Fi module gets to exist. |
| CHT8310 | The temperature/humidity sensor, on U3. |
| Q1 | Switches the CB3S's ground. The MCU only powers the module to send a reading. |
| KEY | A short press makes the MCU reboot the module and send a reading. A long press is Tuya's reset-pairing. |

CB3S pins that matter. Antenna at the bottom: the left edge runs 9 to 16 top to
bottom, the right edge 8 to 1.

| CB3S pin | Signal | Goes to |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | CEN, reset, active low | Pulled up. Ground it to reset. |
| 8 | 3V3 | Straight to BAT+. |
| 9 | GND | Through Q1 to BAT-. Reads about 1.9 MOhm to BAT- while the module is off. |
| 15 | RX1 (P10) | U3's bottom-right pin, the MCU's TX. With U3's dot at top-left, it is the corner pin nearest Q1. |
| 16 | TX1 (P11) | The U3 pin above that one, the MCU's RX. |

The `MCU_TX` / `MCU_RX` test pads near Q1 sit on these two lines.

## The protocol

Tuya's low-power serial protocol: 9600 8N1, frame version byte `0x00`. Not mains
TuyaMCU, not v3 battery mode. OpenBeken's `tmSensor` driver speaks it.

| # | Direction | Frame | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | module to MCU | `55 aa 00 01 00 00 00` | Product info, please. |
| 2 | MCU to module | `55 aa 00 01 00 24` `{"p":"lf36y5nwb8jkxwgg","v":"1.0.0"}` | The MCU's product id. |
| 3 | module to MCU | `55 aa 00 02 00 01 03 05`, then `... 04 06` | On the router. Then on the cloud. |
| 4 | MCU to module | `55 aa 00 02 00 00 01` | Ack. |
| 5 | MCU to module | `55 aa 00 08 ...` three times | The readings, as timestamped records. |
| 6 | module to MCU | `55 aa 00 08 00 01 00 08` | Ack. The MCU cuts the module's power. |

The readings, each in a cmd `0x08` record (a 7-byte time header, then one data
point):

| DP | Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 27 | value | temperature times 10 | 284 = 28.4 C |
| 46 | value | humidity, percent | 42 |
| 101 | enum | battery: 0 low, 1 medium, 2 high | 2 on a bench supply |

**One product id, two DP maps.** Older forum templates for this sensor use DP 1,
2 and 3. That is a different board under the same MCU product id, so do not
match a TH01 by product id alone. This board's 27/46/101 map has been public
since February 2025.

The MCU sends nothing until the module claims step 3's `0x04`, and a stock unit
that has never been paired never says that. The DPs cannot be sniffed off stock
firmware. Flash first, then read OpenBeken's log.

## Why the MCU blocks a normal flash

This is the part every forum thread describes as "the MCU interferes" and stops.
The measurement is the useful part.

U3 drives CB3S pin 15 **push-pull at about 3.1 V**. A USB-serial adapter's TX
cannot drag that line below about 2.9 V, and the BK7231N needs roughly 1 V or
less to read a zero. So the bootloader never hears a link check, and the flash
tool reports no device.

That turns "cut a trace or desolder something" into **lift exactly one pin**: U3's
TX, the bottom-right pin. Half a millimetre of clearance is enough. Pin 15 stops
beeping to it, and the adapter can drive the line.

Two other things follow from the same design:

- **Q1 can switch the module off mid-flash.** A wire from CB3S pin 9 to BAT-
  bypasses it. Remove that wire before the unit goes back in its case, or the MCU
  cannot power the module down and the batteries last days.
- **The MCU grabs pin 15 within milliseconds of power-up.** Power-cycling the
  board while probing does not help.

## Flashing by wire

About an hour with the right adapter, most of a day without one.

1. **Batteries out.** Bench supply at 3.3 V, limit 500 mA or more, into BAT+ and
   BAT-. Add a wire from CB3S pin 9 to BAT- so Q1 cannot switch the module off.
   Pin 8 to pin 9 reads 3.3 V.
2. **Lift U3's bottom-right pin**, the MCU's TX. See above for why.
3. **Adapter: an ESP32 DevKit** (CP2102 on board) with **EN jumpered to GND**,
   which keeps the ESP32 off its own UART pins. Wire it straight through. The
   DevKit's labels are from the ESP32's point of view.

   | DevKit pin | CB3S |
   |---|---|
   | GND | pin 9 / BAT- |
   | RX (GPIO3, driven by the CP2102's TX) | pin 15, RX1 |
   | TX (GPIO1, read by the CP2102's RX) | pin 16, TX1 |
   | 0 (IO0) | pin 1, CEN |

   IO0 is the reset. The DevKit's auto-reset circuit pulls it low on DTR asserted
   and DTR released, and the board's pull-up holds CEN high the rest of the time.
   No 3V3 or 5V wire.

   This wiring is why stock `ltchiptool` cannot drive it: pyserial asserts DTR and
   RTS on every port open, and `ltchiptool` reopens the port after its own reset,
   so CEN is low exactly while it probes. The wrapper in `tools/` streams link
   checks through the CEN pulse instead.
4. **Stop any service that sweeps COM ports.** On Windows, a security suite's
   port monitor can hold them at random and produce a misleading
   `Access is denied`.
5. **Back it up** with a resumable, block-checked reader. Every 4K block is
   CRC-checked against the chip, and it resumes if the chip drops out.
6. **Pull the Tuya config out** with `bk7231tools dissect_dump -e --storage`. The
   storage JSON has the firmware version and the baud rate.
7. **Write OpenBeken.** Use the `UA` image: it goes to `0x11000` and leaves the
   stock bootloader alone, so serial recovery always works. Catching the
   bootloader took 15 pulses once; the wrapper keeps pulsing until it does.
8. **Configure it** with the pin still lifted.
9. **Take the adapter's RX wire off pin 15, then resolder U3's pin.** That wire
   fights the MCU the same way the MCU fought the adapter. Wake the MCU with KEY
   and read the DPs.
10. **Remove the pin 9 bypass wire** before reassembly.

### What did not work

| Tried | Why it failed |
|---|---|
| A USB-C FT232RL board | Weak outputs. DTR reached about 2 V unloaded and could not pull CEN below 2.15 V. TX lost to the MCU. |
| Any adapter with U3's pin still soldered | The MCU holds pin 15 at about 3 V. Link checks never land. |
| Power-cycling the board while probing | The MCU grabs pin 15 within milliseconds of power-up. |
| Manual CEN taps | Caught the bootloader twice. The chip kept rebooting mid-read until the DevKit held CEN up firmly. |
| Stock `ltchiptool` with the IO0 wire | pyserial asserts DTR/RTS on every open, and `ltchiptool` reopens the port after its reset. |

## Cloudcutter

Works, and it is faster than the wire route once the rig is set up. Two units
exploited cleanly on the first try at stock firmware `swv 2.1.8`.

**The profile is not sold as "TH01" anywhere in the cloudcutter database.** It is
catalogued as `tuya-generic-zy-th02pro-temperature-and-humidity-with-display-v2.1.8`,
found by grepping `device-profiles/` for the stock firmware key and product key.
The name says "with Display"; this board has none, which is why nobody searching
for a TH01 finds it.

**Not patched.** Cloudcutter issue #454 closed a TH01 on firmware `2.1.0` as
patched. These units are on `2.1.8`, and both exploited cleanly.

**Power, not the exploit, is the hard part.** The MCU cuts the module's power on
its own schedule. During the run the CB3S has no reason to signal the MCU to cut
power, so it can stay up well past a minute, but only if the KEY keeps getting
pressed every 4 to 5 seconds to stop the MCU's idle timeout. Losing that cadence
for a few seconds drops the radio mid-handshake and the device falls back to
re-broadcasting its own AP. Cloudcutter does not notice and hangs.

**Weak signal is a second, separate failure mode**, specific to the firmware
transfer. A unit that got through the AP-mode exploit fine can still stall
indefinitely once the image starts pushing, even while still link-layer
associated. One unit stalled for over three minutes at -72 dBm and finished in
under 15 seconds at -60 dBm. Move the device next to the adapter before the
transfer starts, not as a last resort.

## OpenBeken setup

The plain `OpenBK7231N` build. `tmSensor` lives inside the TuyaMCU driver, and
the default build has it. A `BK7231T` build bricks a `BK7231N`.

| Step | How |
|---|---|
| Drivers and DP mapping | `POST /api/lfs/autoexec.bat` |
| Flags | `SetFlag 7 1` and `SetFlag 35 1` |
| Name | `FriendlyName`, **before** discovery |
| Discovery | `scheduleHADiscovery 1` |

- **Flag 7** retains everything, so Home Assistant has the last reading after a
  restart. **Flag 35** leaves the availability topic out of discovery, so the
  entities do not sit at "unavailable" between wake-ups. On batteries the module
  is off almost all the time, so both matter.
- **The name matters more than it looks.** OpenBeken builds the HA device id and
  every `unique_id` from the first word of `FriendlyName`. A generic name gives
  every unit of this model the same ids. Changing `FriendlyName` on a unit that
  has already been discovered creates a **second** HA device rather than renaming
  the first, and the old one's entities go stale.
- **`scheduleHADiscovery` is a no-op unless the device is MQTT-connected at that
  exact moment.** It is not a persistent "publish on next connect" flag. Calling
  it over the setup AP, or racing a battery wake window, does nothing, silently.
  Publishing the discovery payloads directly over MQTT, retained, avoids needing
  to catch the device awake at all.
- **`dev.name` is what HA displays; `dev.ids` is the internal key.** Copying an
  existing discovery payload and swapping only `dev.ids` leaves the old `dev.name`
  in place, so the device shows up under its old name regardless.

## On batteries

A wake takes about 22 s from the MCU powering the module to the reading reaching
MQTT. Then the MCU cuts the power. Stock Tuya does it in 5 to 10 s. OpenBeken's
fast connect (a static IP plus flags 37 and 51) would close most of that gap, at
the cost of a DHCP reservation and a cached access-point channel.
